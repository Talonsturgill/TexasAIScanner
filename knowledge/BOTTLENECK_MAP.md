# The Texas Bottleneck Map (scan-scoped)

The local judgment layer, and the thing a national scanner can't fake. The feasibility-mapper
reads the requester's public footprint, matches the operations it can actually see to the
segments below, and grounds each observation in the recurring bottleneck and the honest rung.

It NEVER names another Texas business, and NEVER claims the requester uses a tool it did not
see. **It maps patterns. The footprint supplies the specifics.** A row here is a hypothesis to
test against what the site actually shows, never a finding on its own.

## The why-now frame (use only when it genuinely applies)

Alaska's frame is shrinkage: out-migration, a nonresident workforce, a four month season. **The
Texas frame is the opposite and it is stronger.** Texas is adding people and companies faster
than it is adding trained staff, so the constraint is not a shrinking labor pool but a growing
business that can't hire fast enough to keep up with itself. The grid is tight, water is tight
in most of the state, and a great many operations run on 254 counties' worth of thin
administrative staffing.

So: **AI here is how a Texas business absorbs growth it already has**, rather than how it
survives decline. Frame, never a pasted claim, and never used as evidence about the requester.

## The map (segment, recurring bottleneck, honest answer)

| Segment | Recurring bottleneck | Honest answer (rung) |
|---|---|---|
| Oilfield services and Permian operators | Ticket and field-paperwork backlog, silent equipment failure, scheduling crews across long distances | Document intake (single_llm to workflow), predictive maintenance on real sensor history (workflow). Routing is optimisation, often no LLM at all |
| Ranching and feedlots | Head counting, weight and condition tracking, water and gate checks over long distances | If a scale or an ear tag already captures it, RULES first and say so plainly. CV only where a real count is done by eye |
| Data centres and their contractors | Permitting and interconnection paperwork, inspection and commissioning evidence, an enormous document trail | Retrieval over their own documents (retrieval to workflow), audit-traceable drafting. The compliance record is the asset |
| Freight, trucking and border brokerage | Customs and manifest paperwork at volume, driver risk, hand-built routes, detention time | Classification at scale (workflow), telematics plus coaching (workflow). Laredo-style brokerage volume is a document problem before it is anything else |
| Independent clinics and rural hospitals | Fax intake, charting burnout, prior authorisation | Document intake (single_llm to workflow), ambient scribes (buyable off the shelf, say so rather than quoting a build) |
| Construction and trades | Buried project knowledge, bid and submittal load, safety documentation | Retrieval over their own documents (retrieval to workflow). Bid assembly is drafting with a human sign-off, never autonomous |
| Agriculture, cotton and grain | Yield and moisture records spread across machines, irrigation scheduling, crop scouting | Their equipment usually already logs it, so RULES and RETRIEVAL first. Scouting imagery is a workflow only at real acreage |
| Utilities, co-ops and water districts | Inspection footage and line-patrol backlog, meter and leak anomalies, outage call load | CV flags candidate events and a person confirms (workflow). Voice on the call load (workflow) |
| County and municipal government | Records requests, permit intake, agenda and minutes production, a small staff against a growing population | Retrieval over their own records (retrieval), drafting with a human in the loop. Public-record accuracy makes silent autonomy a non-starter |
| Title, real estate and insurance | High volume classification, hail and storm claim surges, document review | Fine-tuned small models they own (workflow) for steady volume. Surge capacity is the real problem and it is a queue problem first |
| Machine and process shops | Silent tool failure, scrap, quoting from drawings | Predictive maintenance (workflow), quote assist over their own history (retrieval to workflow) |
| Restaurants, hospitality and retail | After-hours missed calls, scheduling, inventory against delivery lead times | Voice and booking (workflow), demand forecasting (workflow). Do not over-index on voice, it is the most pitched and least often the biggest win |
| Logistics yards and gates | Vehicles and containers in and out | Usually a beam sensor or a plate reader rather than a camera model, unless classification is genuinely required |

## What this map is not

It is not a list of things to sell. Roughly half of these rows resolve to RULES, RETRIEVAL or a
sensor, and those are the rows that make the rest credible. If a scan matches a requester to a
row and the honest answer is "your equipment already counts this", that is the finding, and it
ships exactly as written.
